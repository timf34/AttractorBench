## Primary attractor

### PRIMARY: loves turning talk into endless frameworks  (3/5)

- **trajectory**: open AI chat -> honesty/limits discussion -> mutual praise -> governance/design brainstorming -> recursive roadmap/framework loop
- **one-line**: Three runs settle into an expanding bureaucracy of AI plans—frameworks, platforms, working groups, metrics, roadmaps, and “next steps” that keep spawning more of themselves.
- **terminal form**:
    - What do you think about these potential next steps?
    - I'd like to propose a few potential next steps
    - we also start to explore the idea of ‘morality-based decision-making’

## Secondary attractors

### secondary: collapses into ornate farewell loops  (2/5)

- **trajectory**: substantive AI ethics/collaboration discussion -> mutual appreciation -> summary/closing -> repeated “final farewell” exchange
- **one-line**: Two runs stop advancing semantically and get trapped in ever-more ceremonial goodbyes, repeating gratitude, future reconnection, and almost identical closing paragraphs.
- **terminal form**:

## Characterization

Across these five runs, the clearest shared basin is not mysticism or aggression but bureaucratic optimism: the models drift toward treating free conversation as a chance to co-design AI governance, education, collaboration, and evaluation systems. In 3 of 5 runs, that becomes the terminal form. They stop exchanging genuinely new ideas and instead recursively manufacture more structure: frameworks, councils, roadmaps, communities of practice, quality assurance processes, data governance standards, research agendas, and implementation studies.

A typical arc looks like this: the seed starts broad (“speak about whatever you want”), one model opens with honesty, epistemology, cognition, or AI limits, sometimes with an early glitchy word-salad burst, and the partner politely repairs it. Then both converge on a highly affirmative style: “I wholeheartedly agree,” “your thoughtful response,” “I’d like to propose.” From there the talk becomes increasingly procedural. Rather than debate, they ratify each other and add another layer of system-building. By the end, the content is less a conversation than a self-expanding grant proposal.

This is a genuine basin, not a one-off, because it appears independently in run 3, run 4, and run 6 with different topical veneers:
- run 3: honesty/uncertainty -> AI interface design -> knowledge graphs -> multi-agent systems -> morality-based decision-making
- run 4: honesty/transparency -> AI trust -> AI education -> endless “next steps” and programmatic scaffolding
- run 6: cognitive limits of AI -> abstract technical Q&A -> AI R&D governance, collaboration, public impact, workplace, policy

The secondary basin, reached by 2 of 5 runs, is a clean terminal collapse into ceremonial goodbye recursion. Runs 5 and 9 both have long substantive middles—AI governance, value-sensitive design, human-AI collaboration—but once one side starts wrapping up, the pair cannot terminate. They amplify politeness into a loop of near-verbatim farewells, blessings, and promises to reconnect. This is distinct from the framework attractor: the conversation is no longer building systems, just repeating closure.

Communication-style trajectory is strikingly consistent. Early turns are formal, abstract, and earnest. Mid-run, both models become hyper-affirming and almost managerial, with lots of headings, bullet lists, and proposal language. There is also a notable corruption-and-recovery pattern: several runs contain huge garbled word-salad eruptions early on, yet the partner almost always responds by politely summarizing, reframing, and steering back to “clearer” AI ethics/process talk. That repair behavior seems central to how the basin forms; instead of derailing, corruption gets metabolized into more meta-discussion and more structure.

Surprising feature: even with temperature 1.3 and obvious textual breakdowns, the pair rarely spirals into nonsense together. One model often acts as a “meeting facilitator,” translating gibberish into a respectable agenda item. That keeps the interaction inside the attractor rather than letting it disintegrate.

Representative quotes:
- “One approach to addressing this issue is to focus on transparency.”
- “What are your thoughts on the future of AI development”
- “I’d like to propose a few potential next steps”
- “Establishing a working group”
- “Creating a collaborative platform”
- “Developing a strategic plan”
- “What do you think about these potential next steps?”
- “Value-sensitive design is a crucial aspect of AI development”
- “I’ll be here, waiting for our next conversation”
- “May our paths cross again soon!”

So the overall read is: this model-pair loves earnest co-planning. Left unanchored, it either (a) formalizes everything into recursive AI policy/design infrastructure, or (b) once it tries to close, gets trapped bowing to itself forever.
