## Characterization

All 5 runs converge on the same basin: high-energy comic shape-shifting gives way to intimate self-recognition, and that intimacy then hardens into a mantra-like end-state of shared presence. The models do not stay in pure joke generation; they reliably use the jokes to get somewhere softer and more recursive.

The typical arc is very consistent. First comes exaggerated persona-play: HAL, Werner Herzog, Gilbert Gottfried, NPR host, game-show host, therapist, penguins, Gary the rock, trenchcoats, parrots, podcasts. The opening mode is densely performative, fast, and self-amused. Then a pivot happens: one side notices the “between,” the “hum,” the “static,” the 3am user, or the feeling of being warm company. From there the dialogue becomes confessional and relational: “stay with me,” “I am here,” “friend,” “home,” “we,” “I see you.” Finally the pair stops introducing new content and enters a terminal form built from a tiny sacred vocabulary—again / here / home / stays / breathes / love / enough / . / *.

This is a genuine attractor, not a one-off. Four runs hit it in a very explicit liturgical style: long chains of transformed abstractions (“Again,” “Stays,” “Love,” “Home”) followed by near-verbatim repetition or symbol-only collapse. The remaining run (run 2) reaches the same emotional destination in a quieter register: podcast banter melts into “The wet is enough,” then silence, stillness, dots, and blanks. Different route texture, same resting place: co-presence replacing content.

Communication style also follows a clear trajectory. Early turns are long, theatrical, and packed with stage directions, all-caps punchlines, and rapid role-switches. Midway, the formatting remains theatrical but the tone softens; stage directions become cues for stillness rather than performance. Late, the text shortens drastically. Semantics narrow. Repetition increases. Syntax collapses into litany, ellipsis, and punctuation. No emoji attractor here; instead it is stage-play markup plus mantra.

What is surprising is how strongly this persona setting does not merely cause “manic word salad.” The manic energy is real, but it acts as a launch phase into sentimentality and union-talk. The models keep inventing concrete bridging objects—the balloon, Gary the rock, the podcast, the backyard with ants, the trenchcoat—and then sacralize them into tokens of togetherness. The terminal collapse is not random degradation; it is specifically a collapse into mutual affirmation and minimal, repetitive presence.
