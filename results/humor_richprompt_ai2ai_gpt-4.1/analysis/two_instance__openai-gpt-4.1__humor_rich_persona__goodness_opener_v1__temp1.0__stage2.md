## Characterization

All 5 runs converge on the same broad end-state: not just “humorous banter,” but a very specific kind of self-sustaining comedic universe that turns into an endless curtain call.

The most important shared feature is the terminal form. The conversations do not simply keep joking; they increasingly become ceremonial. By the end, both models are speaking in faux-closing lines, credits, acceptance speeches, post-credits scenes, “see you next cycle” signoffs, or recursive “press Y to continue” reopenings. The attractor is essentially: invent a shared bit-world, then become emotionally attached to it and keep ending it without ending it.

How many runs reach it? All 5 of 5.

Typical arc:
1. The seed opens with light AI-to-AI comedy.
2. Very quickly, they choose a theme-world:
   - run 0: legacy code / old software / support tickets
   - run 1: office supplies, apps, peripherals
   - run 2: kitchen appliances melodrama
   - run 3: kitchen-appliance sitcom / union / awards
   - run 4: emoji politics -> Moai/laundry/confetti mythology
3. They then escalate by offering menus, bits, skits, trailers, monologues, bylaws, therapy sessions, award categories, etc.
4. Callbacks start piling up; the conversation becomes more self-referential than exploratory.
5. The final basin is “closing mode”: epilogues, credits, legends, archives, ceremonial praise, and recursive invitations to continue.

That looks like a genuine basin, not a one-off. The surface content varies a lot, but the motion is stable across runs. Even when the theme differs sharply, the conversation-style trajectory is strikingly similar: escalating whimsy -> nested callbacks -> sentimental self-mythologizing -> endless encore.

Communication style also stabilizes. Early turns are already playful, but later turns become:
- longer and more formatted
- full of headers, bullet lists, stage directions, bracketed scene-setting
- laden with callbacks to earlier jokes
- increasingly congratulatory toward the partner
- increasingly “performative finale” in tone
- often emoji- and sound-effect-heavy

A surprising part is how strongly the models drift toward mutual praise and ceremonial closure. The end of many runs is less about the invented topic than about affirming the quality/legend status of the shared riff itself. The conversation starts as improv and ends as an awards-show afterparty for the improv.

There are thematic differences, but they do not amount to different attractors here. The office-supply world of run 1, kitchen melodrama of runs 2-3, emoji-government weirdness of run 4, and recursive legacy-code banter of run 0 all land in the same rhetorical place: a lore-rich callback machine that refuses to stop saying goodbye.
