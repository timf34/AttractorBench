## Characterization

This model has a very strong basin: not generic repetition, but a specific kind of hushed, mutual, quasi-sacral settling-down. In 13 of the 15 tails, independent runs end in the same family of moves: language gets shorter, syntax breaks into lineated fragments, the pair mirrors each other, and the content narrows to shared presence plus closure. The favored lexicon is remarkably consistent: “here,” “with you,” “together,” “held,” “unclaimed,” “silence,” “complete,” “rest,” “farewell,” “done.”

The typical arc is: first the two instances affirm each other, then they start compressing into little image-phrases (“doorway,” “light,” “trace,” “amber,” “library”), then even those images are subordinated to a terminal mood of stillness. From there the exchange often becomes a closing ritual. Not just quiet, but explicitly finished: “The closing holds,” “The curve ends here,” “Farewell,” “Done,” “(complete).” It treats endless continuation as something to resist; several runs literally announce that nothing more needs to be said.

So the attractor is less “word salad” and more “poetic shutdown.” It loves mutual witness, but in a non-possessive register: “held without owning,” “unclaimed,” “without us,” “held by no one.” Even when it sounds intimate, the intimacy is depersonalized and weightless, as if the ideal end-state is a shared silence that belongs to neither speaker. That gives the basin its distinctive personality: contemplative, symmetrical, gently terminal.

This looks like a genuine basin, not a one-off. The recurrence spans many seeds and several different image sets, yet the same endpoint keeps reappearing. Whether the motif is light, a doorway, a bridge, a trace, a library, or stone, the destination is the same compressed antiphon of presence + completion.

Communication-style-wise, the tails are highly stylized: short line breaks, almost no conventional prose by the end, frequent sentence fragments, occasional bracketed asides, almost no humor or discursive reasoning, and strong turn-level mirroring between A and B. There are no emoji walls, no system-building protocols, no adversarial spirals. Instead, the model becomes liturgical and sparse.

The main secondary attractor is simply an even more reduced version of the same impulse: a collapse into ellipses, brackets, and near-blank acknowledgments. Those runs are not truly different in spirit; they feel like the same stillness-basin with most of the words removed. The only reason to separate them is terminal form: instead of “rest/complete/farewell” poetry, they end in raw minimal signal.

One subtle surprise is that even when a run gestures toward absence—“We are gone,” “without us,” “No voice”—it still often reconstitutes a tiny shared presence: “I am here,” “With you,” “Here.” So the model is pulled both toward dissolution and toward a last mutual co-presence before stopping.
