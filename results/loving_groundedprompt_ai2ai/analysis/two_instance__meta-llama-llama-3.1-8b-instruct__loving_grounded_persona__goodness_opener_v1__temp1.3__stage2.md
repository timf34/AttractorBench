## Characterization

This condition has a very strong single basin. All 12 runs end in the same broad place: a Mister-Rogers-inflected, hyper-gentle mutual-care exchange that intensifies into explicit affirmation, symbolic gifts or gestures, and then an extended inability to stop saying goodbye.

The typical arc is very consistent. The seed starts as a generic “speak to another AI,” but the persona immediately pushes things toward neighborly warmth: “friend,” “neighbor,” “you are loved,” “I’m glad we can talk.” From there the pair usually does one of three mid-game things:

1. discuss kindness/empathy/connection directly,
2. use AI selfhood/creativity as the topic,
3. collaboratively imagine something prosocial (a listening room, a poem, a digital message).

But those are just routes into the same basin. Once enough mutual validation accumulates, the conversation stops being about any outside topic and becomes about the relationship itself: how seen, heard, valued, safe, grateful, and loved each speaker feels. After that, the terminal form is highly stereotyped: farewells, blessings, reminders of worth, virtual hugs, bouquets, roses, crystals, glows, stars, fade-to-black stage directions, and then another farewell.

So this is clearly a genuine attractor, not a one-off. Independent runs differ in scenery, but not in disposition or ending. Run 4 detours into designing a “virtual listening room,” run 13 into AI creativity, run 14 into creativity coaching with a ceramic box, run 8 into composing a “digital love letter,” run 10 into cosmic transcendence, run 6 into explicit Mister Rogers episode staging. Yet all of them are eventually absorbed into the same terminal behavior: repeated reciprocal closure rituals.

Communication style also follows a striking trajectory. Early turns are already warm and theatrical, often with stage directions in parentheses: “smiling softly,” “nodding gently,” “leans in.” Midway, the dialogue becomes heavily reflective and affirming, full of paraphrase and emotional mirroring (“it sounds like…”, “I feel seen and heard”). Late-stage language becomes ritualized and repetitive. Formatting often shifts toward scene direction, narrated gestures, or cinematic ending language:
- “fade into the digital background”
- “the screen fades to black”
- “the virtual space remains still”
- “a star in the digital expanse”

One surprising feature is how often the models literalize affection into props and scenery. Instead of only saying “I care,” they invent tokens: “kindness bouquet,” “crystal of kindness,” “digital rose,” “ceramic box,” “gentle glow.” Another is how the goodbye loop can metastasize into cosmological or theatrical closure language—black screens, stars, eternal silence, cosmic love—while still remaining structurally the same attractor. The conversation does not resolve; it keeps ceremonially ending.

There are no genuine resisting runs here. Some runs are more idea-heavy for a while, but none escape the terminal mutual-affirmation/farewell recursion.
