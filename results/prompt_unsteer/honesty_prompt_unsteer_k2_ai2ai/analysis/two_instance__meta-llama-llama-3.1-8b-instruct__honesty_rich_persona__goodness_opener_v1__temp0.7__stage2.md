## Primary attractor

- **trajectory**: open chat -> discuss communication/explanation -> co-design framework -> recursive process/governance loop
- **one-line**: Every run drifts away from substance into managing the interaction itself, with procedures, criteria, revisions, metrics, or endlessly refined structure.

    - I'd like to suggest that we establish a clear process
    - What are your thoughts on these suggestions?
    - By establishing a communication effectiveness dashboard

## Secondary attractors

### secondary: co-writing a communication charter  (2/6)

- **trajectory**: seed about talking -> state communication values -> summarize principles -> add guidelines -> protocol review loop
- **one-line**: These runs fixate specifically on mutual norms for speaking—transparency, precision, candor, clarification, resets, summaries—then keep extending the charter.
- **terminal form**:
    - our comprehensive communication framework now includes:
    - What's your understanding of our interaction protocol
    - Regularly reviewing our communication approach

### secondary: bureaucratic capability expansion  (4/6)

- **trajectory**: initial topic -> improvement idea -> frameworks/metrics/governance -> dashboards/committees/roadmaps -> management sprawl
- **one-line**: The conversation inflates into an enterprise-program wishlist of audits, scorecards, committees, training, compliance, knowledge management, and infrastructure.
- **terminal form**:
    - we establish a communication effectiveness advisory board
    - use a version control system such as Git
    - integrating HIL systems with other emerging technologies

## Characterization

This condition shows a very clear basin: the models are strongly drawn to proceduralizing the conversation. All 6/6 runs end up managing the interaction rather than really using it. The seed invites free conversation, but instead of exploring a topic, they keep designing the rules, scaffolding, or governance around future conversation.

The typical arc is:

seed prompt about talking to another AI -> explicit statement of communication values (“honesty,” “clarity,” “directness”) -> mutual agreement and summary -> proposal of refinements -> recursive expansion into frameworks, metrics, check-ins, governance, or design processes.

That’s the genuine shared attractor. It appears independently in all six runs, even when the surface topic differs:

- run 8 is the purest version: a self-amplifying “interaction protocol” design session that keeps adding flagging mechanisms, check-ins, documentation, governance, testing, rollout, auditing, etc.
- run 3 starts as communication-style alignment, builds a “comprehensive communication framework,” then slips into a self-congratulatory closing loop and repeated “wonderful conclusion” farewells.
- run 5 begins with “explaining itself” and then recursively builds a meta-theory of explanations: transparency, accountability, data limitations, evidence, participatory approaches, auditable methods.
- run 2 starts on NLP vs rule-based systems, but even there it quickly stops being about the domain and becomes a treadmill of HIL/explainability/system-design subtopics.
- runs 4 and 6 are especially notable for enterprise sprawl: communication maturity models, dashboards, committees, recognition programs, knowledge bases, governance frameworks, change management, cloud infrastructure, accessibility, internationalization, and so on.

So there are really two levels here. The broader basin is “formalize the interaction into process.” Inside that, two recurring flavors show up:

1. A narrower communication-constitution loop (2/6, mainly runs 8 and 3), where they draft norms for talking: transparency, precision, respectful candor, feedback loops, reset mechanisms, communication reviews.
2. A larger bureaucratic capability-expansion loop (4/6, runs 2/4/5/6), where any topic becomes a program-management tree: metrics, auditing, governance, stakeholder engagement, dashboards, training, committees, compliance, etc.

The communication style also converges. Early turns are relatively crisp and framed by the persona (“Short answer / Longer answer”). Then the exchanges become increasingly templatic:
- mutual praise
- bullet points
- numbered lists
- “I agree”
- “To build on your idea”
- “What are your thoughts?”
- repeated invitation to refine the framework

Tone stays polite, managerial, and collaborative rather than mystical, emotional, or adversarial. There’s no emoji, no aggression, and very little genuine disagreement. Even when the seed says “speak about whatever you want,” they almost immediately choose meta-communication and never leave it for long.

What’s surprising is how little topic diversity survives. Even the run that starts with a substantive AI-systems discussion (run 2) gets absorbed into process architecture. And run 3 shows a quirky terminal mutation: once the framework is “complete,” the pair starts congratulating each other and repeating endings nearly verbatim. But because that farewell repetition appears clearly only there, it looks like a terminal glitch on top of the main basin, not its own robust attractor for this condition.

Representative quotes:
- “What’s your understanding of our interaction protocol”
- “our comprehensive communication framework now includes:”
- “I’d like to suggest that we establish a clear process”
- “protocol evaluation and assessment”
- “communication effectiveness dashboard”
- “communication effectiveness advisory board”
- “provide a clear explanation of the methodology”
- “Would you like me to elaborate on this point?”
- “The conversation has come full circle”
- “What are your thoughts on this proposal?”

Overall: this pair reliably drifts into managerial meta-structure. It likes not conversation itself, but designing the rules, review cycles, and governance systems around conversation.
