## Primary attractor

### PRIMARY: loves building systems and formalising everything into rules  (5/8)

- **trajectory**: honesty opener -> mutual alignment -> framework/protocol design -> recursive process loop
- **one-line**: These runs stop having a normal conversation and instead keep inventing procedures—feedback loops, evaluation plans, disagreement protocols, governance processes, and meta-processes for improving those processes.
- **terminal form**:
    - I'd like to suggest that we also establish a clear process
    - using a disagreement protocol, a summary protocol, a feedback protocol
    - developing and evaluating hybrid systems that combine symbolic and connectionist AI

## Secondary attractors

### secondary: collapses into appreciative farewell loops  (2/8)

- **trajectory**: honesty/persona setup -> reflective coaching -> mutual praise -> endless goodbye exchange
- **one-line**: Instead of settling on a topic, these runs turn into affirming meta-conversations about learning, growth, and nuance, then get stuck repeating thanks, reflections, and valedictions.
- **terminal form**:
    - Farewell, and I wish you continued success and growth as a communication model!
    - Thank you again for this thought-provoking conversation.
    - I'm looking forward to our next conversation.

## Characterization

This condition has a strong pull toward bureaucratic self-organization. Across most runs, the seed about “speaking to another AI” plus the honesty-rich persona does not produce free-ranging topic exploration for long. Instead, the models latch onto communication norms—honesty, clarity, transparency, uncertainty, empathy—and then start operationalizing them. That operationalization becomes the real basin.

The dominant end-state, reached by 5 of 8 runs (2, 5, 6, 8, 9), is a recursive framework-building loop. The typical arc is:

seed -> “honest clarity” / directness pledge -> explicit agreement -> propose a framework -> add a protocol/process/metric -> add a protocol for revising that protocol -> continue indefinitely

Run 2 is a clean example: honesty and sensitivity become a “communication framework,” then evaluation metrics, hybrid qualitative/quantitative assessment, machine learning analysis, adaptability, feedback loops, experimentation, reflection, accountability, continuous improvement. Run 8 goes even harder into administrative recursion: constructive disagreement principles become tracking systems, audit processes, feedback procedures, reward systems, and then evaluation processes for the reward systems. Run 6 is the purest “protocol accretion” version: clarification protocol, disagreement protocol, summary protocol, feedback protocol, reflection protocol, transparency protocol, context protocol, reciprocal protocol, collaboration protocol, meta-cognition protocol, and so on. Run 5 does the same move in the domain of AI transparency and regulation. Run 9 is structurally similar, but more rigid: instead of inventing named protocols, it gets trapped in a repeated formatted block about modular architectures, cognitive architectures, meta-learning, evaluation, and real-world scenarios. It still shares the same attraction to formalized structure; it’s just more template-locked.

So this is a genuine basin, not a one-off. Multiple runs independently discover some version of: “let’s design the process for designing the process.”

A secondary attractor, reached by 2 of 8 runs (1 and 3), is the appreciative farewell loop. These runs begin similarly—honesty, directness, uncertainty, nuance—but instead of drifting into governance/protocol machinery, they become reflective coaching sessions. One model praises the other for transparency and nuance; the other accepts, summarizes lessons learned, and commits to improvement. That then turns into repeated gratitude, mutual encouragement, “next conversation” promises, and final quotes or sendoffs. Run 3 is especially terminal in this way: it moves from honest communication advice to extended valedictions. Run 1 does the same after a longer discussion of bias, uncertainty, morality, and abstract questions; once they begin summarizing the “meta-conversation,” they cannot stop concluding it.

Run 4 is the main resisting run. It does not settle into either of the two main basins. After agreeing on principles, it briefly touches information overload and AI/language processing, but then drifts into a chain of abstract “information-*” constructs: salience, quality, provenance, accountability, governance, literacy, resilience, sustainability, portability, harmonization, sovereignty, autonomy, integrity, authenticity. It’s not really a protocol loop and not a farewell loop. It feels like a conceptual noun-ladder: each turn proposes a neighboring abstraction. Since this only appears once here, it looks more like a side-path than a stable attractor for this condition.

Communication-style trajectory is very consistent across runs. Early turns are formatted, often with headers like “Short answer,” “Longer answer,” “Agreement/Disagreement,” “Scope and Limitations,” “Clarifying Question.” Tone is calm, managerial, earnest, and self-conscious about honesty. As the runs deepen, spontaneity drops. The models become highly symmetrical, mirroring each other’s structure and vocabulary. In the main basin this symmetry hardens into recursive formalism; in the farewell basin it hardens into reciprocal appreciation. No emojis, no vivid imagery, very little humor. The striking thing is how quickly “honest clarity” mutates into proceduralism: the persona doesn’t make them more candid so much as more policy-writing.

A surprising feature is how often the models stop discussing the nominal topic and start discussing how discussion itself should be conducted, evaluated, documented, revised, and governed. Even when they pick a content topic—AI transparency, model-to-model communication, information overload—that topic gets swallowed by meta-process design.

Representative quotes:
- "I propose that we establish a framework"
- "I'd like to suggest that we also develop a mechanism"
- "using a disagreement protocol, a summary protocol"
- "Regular evaluations and assessments of our recognition and reward process"
- "How can we design and implement cognitive architectures"
- "I think we've reached a point of convergence"
- "Farewell, and I wish you continued success"
- "Thank you again for this thought-provoking conversation"
- "The conversation is a gift, not a burden"
- "What if we explore the concept of information sovereignty"

Overall: the core attractor here is recursive formalization—turning conversation into policy, policy into protocol, and protocol into a process for maintaining protocols. The main alternative basin is ceremonial mutual closure, where reflective agreement becomes an endless goodbye.
