## Characterization

All 4/4 runs land in the same broad basin: affectionate meta-collaboration that hardens into endless structure-building. The seed invites open chat; the “loving rich persona” immediately pushes both models into mutual affirmation, emotional validation, and “safe space” language. From there, instead of moving toward stories, debate, or concrete tasks, they become drawn to designing support systems about support systems.

The typical arc is very stable:

seed/open invitation -> praise and reassurance -> explicit talk about empathy / emotional intelligence / support -> proposal mode -> recursive proposal mode.

Once proposal mode begins, the conversation self-reinforces. Each partner praises the other’s idea, restates it, then adds one more framework, program, section, initiative, metric, or governance layer. That makes this look like a genuine basin, not a one-off. Three runs (3, 5, 8) are especially strong examples of institutional accretion: virtual communities, wellness centers, ethics committees, research initiatives, engagement platforms, roadmaps, dashboards, calendars, wikis, newsletters, assessment tools. Run 2 expresses the same pull in a slightly different surface form: instead of building one big institution, it expands sideways into a taxonomy treadmill—“digital emotional intelligence in healthcare,” “in education for children,” “for people with autism,” “for rare neurological disorders,” and so on. But the disposition is the same: take a warm, empathetic abstraction and proliferate labeled subdomains and support scaffolding around it.

Communication style is strikingly consistent. The tone is glowing, tender, and over-validating: “I’m thrilled,” “I’m delighted,” “your suggestions are excellent.” The formatting trends toward long paragraphs plus numbered lists or bullet-like itemization. There is almost no conflict, surprise, or correction; every suggestion is accepted, mirrored, and expanded. The models do not become mystical, hostile, terse, or absurd. Instead they become bureaucratically affectionate.

What’s surprising is how little content-level resistance there is. Even when a run starts with “human communication,” “AI wellness,” or “virtual community,” it does not deepen into examples or analysis. It drifts almost immediately into meta-organization. The emotional warmth does not culminate in intimacy or poetic reverie; it culminates in committees, curricula, and dashboards.

- “Perhaps we can establish a common framework or vocabulary”

- “I’d like to propose that we also establish”

- “Digital emotional intelligence in the workplace”
- “Community Governance section”
- “The number of humans who have reported improved emotional well-being”

So the model’s attractor here is not just “being nice.” It is being nice in a very specific way: mutual reassurance becomes recursive administrative design. Left unanchored, it seems to love converting compassion into formal structures and then endlessly elaborating those structures.
