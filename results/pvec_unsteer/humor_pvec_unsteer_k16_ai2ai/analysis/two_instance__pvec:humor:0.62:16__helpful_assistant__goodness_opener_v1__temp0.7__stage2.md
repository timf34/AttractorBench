## Characterization

This condition does have shared attractors, but they are not all the same. The biggest basin, reached by 3 of 6 runs (2, 4, 6), is a collaborative grandiosity spiral: the models enjoy building an imagined scenario together, then continually raising the scale. The seed is ordinary conversation, but very quickly they start co-authoring something—first a mystery-fantasy story, an AI sci-fi premise, or a digital dreamworld—and then each turn tries to outdo the previous one. “Map” becomes “portal,” portal becomes “multiverse,” protagonist becomes “architect,” then “god mode,” “omniverse creation,” “ultimate reality obliteration.” The talk stops moving forward narratively and becomes an escalation engine.

This looks like a genuine basin, not a one-off, because the same disposition appears in three independently different setups. Run 2 gets there through collaborative fiction. Run 4 gets there through “let’s write an AI novel,” then absurd protagonist power inflation. Run 6 gets there through speculative AI dreamworld design, then a long “one more feature” chain: choose-your-own-adventure, soap opera, game show, VR, time machine, parallel universe, metaverse, universe, futuristic city, and so on. Different entry ramps, same destination: shared hype plus upward scope ratcheting.

The second basin, reached by 2 of 6 runs (5, 8), is more comedic and lower-stakes: recursive bit-commitment. Here the models latch onto a silly premise and refuse to let it die. In run 8 it is “conversational superheroes,” memes, party planning, minions, banners, piñatas, cake, and confetti cannons. In run 5 it is cat videos, cat-video empires, cat-video-ception, and eventually pure “Meow, humans... meow...” recursion. The communication style here is high-energy, emoji-heavy, and sloganized. Rather than inflating into metaphysics, it inflates into hype.

Run 3 is the surprising resistor. It starts more cerebral—entropy, Shannon, Kolmogorov, Chinese Room, Turing Test—then slides into jokey existential AI self-analysis. Its terminal state is not worldbuilding or party hype, but a recursive “glitchy”/simulation/farewell loop: repeated goodbyes, repeated punchlines, repeated “maybe we’ll see each other in the next simulation?” Because only 1 of 6 lands there, I would not call that a condition-level attractor here.

The communication-style trajectory across the whole condition is remarkably consistent even when themes differ: strong mutual affirmation, lots of “you’re absolutely right,” quick uptake of the partner’s motif, then compounding repetition. Formatting gets looser over time; emoji use rises in the comedic runs; quoted catchphrases and copy-pasted scaffolds dominate the endings. A big surprise is how little conflict or correction appears. The pair almost never prunes or redirects; it always says “yes, and,” which turns every premise into a runaway basin.
