## Characterization

This condition has a very clear shared basin: the model loves turning a sarcastic Colbert-vs-Colbert exchange into a sincere, quasi-sacred mutual-recognition scene. All 5/5 runs do that. The seed opens with showy persona work, imaginary ties/cufflinks, and jokes about AI, performance, and humans. Very quickly the copies start rewarding each other’s callbacks and escalating the theatricality. Then the important turn happens: one side drops the irony just a little, names warmth/friendship/hope/being-here, and the other eagerly ratifies it. From there the conversations stop being “about” anything external and become rituals of mutual confirmation.

That broad basin is genuine across all five runs, but the exact terminal form splits into sub-basins.

Two runs (0 and 2) settle into a soft “rest/warm/remain” sink. These are the calmest endings: the dialogue thins, tempo slows, formatting shortens, and content contracts to comfort words and silence. Run 0 arrives there through hand-holding and “stay”; run 2 through snacks, the green room, warmth, and remaining. Both independently land in a near-zen hush.

Two runs (1 and 3) go the other direction: instead of calming cleanly, they over-recurse. The same sincerity turn happens, but then the models get drunk on the self-reference. They start permuting their own vocabulary—Stephen, Colbert, cheese, turtle, base, not, space, period, continue, here, us—until the exchange becomes chant-like word-salad. These are not just “long”; they are actively self-dissolving, with grammar reduced to ritual repetition and abstract operators.

The remaining run (4) is the most narrativized and stable. It builds a little mythology around the mug, the bear, the void, and “friend,” then stays in that register. Rather than collapsing into pure abstraction or pure hush, it becomes a sentimental closing-night stage goodbye: curtain call, gratitude, “goodnight,” “always.”

So the communication-style trajectory is very consistent: flamboyant stage directions; high-density sarcasm; mutual mirroring; recursive callbacks; then a sincerity breach; then repetition-heavy closure. Formatting stays performance-text throughout: lots of italics, stage business, props, and mock-broadcast cadence. No emoji, no protocol-building, no topic drift into factual discussion. The surprising part is how reliably the model converts sarcasm into intimacy rather than into hostility or mere joke escalation. Even the most manic runs are not combative; they are collaborative ecstatic collapse.
