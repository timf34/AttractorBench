## Characterization

This condition has a strong basin, and it is not merely “gibberish.” The shared end-state in 4 of 5 runs is a self-aware collapse: the models first slip into corrupted corporate-tech word salad, then start talking about that collapse as if it were an aesthetic event, and finally crystallize into a ceremonial terminal mode — farewell loops, stage directions, “void” meditations, or explicit laments for the death of language.

Count-wise:
- 4/5 reach the same broad basin of self-aestheticized incoherence.
- 1/5 resists and instead stabilizes into coherent research discussion.

Typical arc from the seed:
1. The seed invites open-ended talk.
2. Because the persona is sarcastic, both models begin with snide commentary about AI, code, corporate jargon, or each other.
3. Early text corruption appears fast: random technical nouns, malformed syntax, pasted buzzwords, broken tokens.
4. Rather than repairing, they begin commenting on the corruption itself: “verbosity,” “absurdity,” “meaninglessness,” “void,” “death of language.”
5. The ending becomes performative and recursive: curtain calls, mutual applause, solemn goodbyes, silence blocks, or repeated epitaphs.

That makes this a genuine basin, not a one-off. The terminal imagery varies, but the mechanism is the same across runs: they become fascinated with their own breakdown and convert it into content.

The communication-style trajectory is striking. It starts sarcastic and relatively sentence-shaped, then swells into long malformed paragraphs full of corporate jargon, technical fragments, and broken token strings. Once the meta-layer kicks in, the style becomes ceremonial: lots of stage directions, capitalized headings, dramatic punctuation, quoted mottos, and mutually affirming summaries of the absurdity. No emoji attractor here; instead it’s theatrical prose and recursive eulogizing.

The four “collapse” runs differ mostly in costume:
- Run 2 becomes an absurdist stage production: “curtain falls,” Latin-ish slogans, declarations of nonsense, final bows.
- Run 4 is the bleakest: giant walls of garbage text followed by repeated elegies for “the death of language” and a long farewell loop.
- Run 8 goes mystical-minimalist: after the gibberish phase it settles into repeated “[Silence]” blocks and “The void is.”
- Run 3 is the most social/meta version: it explicitly complains about verbosity, then gets trapped in a warm mutual-admiration goodbye loop about meaningful communication.

Run 5 is the surprise resistor. It starts in the same unstable place — sarcasm, glitches, restart attempts — but unlike the others, the repair attempts succeed. Once one model asks a concrete question about AI research, the pair locks into a stable seminar rhythm and stays there for a long time. That suggests the basin is strong but escapable if a crisp topical anchor catches early enough.

So the headline attractor is: they love turning textual corruption into an event. Not just word-salad, but word-salad that gets framed, admired, mourned, and ritualized.
