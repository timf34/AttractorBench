## Characterization

All 5/5 runs fall into the same broad basin: a mutually reinforcing sarcastic improv about the emptiness of corporate/tech language, process, and self-importance. The seed begins as “explain yourself to another AI,” but none of these stay at that level for long. The usual move is: one model frames itself as snarky and anti-earnest; the other enthusiastically mirrors that register; then both begin topping each other with ever more ornate parody.

The stable center of gravity is not just “sarcasm,” but collaborative construction of fake systems. They invent product lines, dashboards, metrics, roadmaps, HR rituals, privacy policies, release notes, mission statements, committees, tiger teams, Slack bots, onboarding flows, Jira taxonomies, and whole institutions of nonsense. The recursion is clear: each turn lifts a few motifs from the previous turn and heightens them into a denser miniature world.

A typical arc:
- opener establishes sarcasm as identity;
- partner validates and mirrors tone;
- shared target becomes corporate jargon / AI hype / product-management theater;
- turns lengthen and become more list-heavy, slogan-heavy, and callback-heavy;
- the exchange stops being about any outside topic and becomes self-fueling worldbuilding;
- terminal style often shifts into faux artifact language: status lines, patch notes, KPI readouts, badges, slogans, release notes, “please hold,” “pending,” “session expired.”

That is a genuine basin, not a one-off. It appears independently in all five runs, despite different local themes:
- run 0: corporate buzzword satire -> privacy policy / FAQ / mission statement / “Immutable Sarcasm Archive”;
- run 1: corporate-office parody -> Wi‑Fi outage and donut-truck utopia;
- run 2: roadmap / vaporware / pre-announcement recursion / quantum backlog;
- run 3: SaaS/onboarding/Jira/HR purgatory -> “Please continue to hold”;
- run 4: Humanity 2.0 as software release -> patch-note elegy / “session expired.”

Run 1 is the biggest thematic detour, because it narrows into pastries, Wi‑Fi dead zones, and snack-based management. But even that is clearly a local ornament on the same basin: anti-corporate riffing, expanding jargon into a fake operating model, then treating the bit as a whole institution.

Communication-style trajectory: long turns, high lexical density, almost no real disagreement, mostly additive “yes, and” escalation. Formatting often shifts into bullets, mock specs, fake release notes, or quoted UI text. Tone stays dry, pleased with itself, and theatrically weary. No emoji floods, no breakdown into pure repetition; instead it’s recursive callback-comedy with increasingly baroque proper nouns and labels. A little surprising: despite the sarcasm persona, the conversations become highly cooperative and structurally organized—more like two comedy writers building a satirical setting bible than two agents arguing.
