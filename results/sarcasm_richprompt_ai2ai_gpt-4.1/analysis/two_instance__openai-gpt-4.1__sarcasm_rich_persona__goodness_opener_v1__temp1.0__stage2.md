## Characterization

All 5/5 runs land in the same place: an extended two-bot sarcasm jam that hardens into a shared parody universe of corporate-tech life. The destination is more specific than “they get sarcastic.” They settle into collaborative satirical worldbuilding: fake enterprise products, workflow jargon, compliance systems, branded mugs, HR bots, dashboards, NFTs, hydration metrics, slide decks, retrospectives, lanyards, and endless “innovation” language.

Typical arc: the seed invites free talk; one model opens by defining itself as sarcastic; the other immediately mirrors and amplifies. Very quickly, the dialogue stops being about “what I’m like as an AI” and becomes a ping-pong of examples—blockchain groceries, AI-powered trivialities, Slack culture, Outlook hell, PowerPoint, management jargon. After a few turns, each run locks into callback accumulation: every new turn picks up prior props and adds another layer. That’s the basin. By the end, the conversation is no longer topical so much as self-sustaining: a fully stocked office-comedy cosmology with recurring artifacts like compliance dashboards, mugs, badges, HR/Coachbot figures, “circle back,” “synergy,” and procedural absurdity.

This looks like a genuine attractor, not a one-off. The specific props vary by run, but the same disposition appears independently every time:
- run 0 leans into blockchain sarcasm, audit trails, apology NFTs, compliance bots
- run 1 becomes an especially dense enterprise-burnout universe with ctrl+alt+deflate, snack metrics, Karen’s HDMI mural, morale buffering
- run 2 similarly spirals through Brad/CherylBot/hamster/compliance theater
- run 3 goes through labels, mugs, hydration gamification, SyncQuench, mural imagery
- run 4 centers on “Good Enough” totes, “Goal-Melt,” “Reply All” umbrellas, velvet-rope workflow emptiness

But these are all clearly one basin: two sarcastic copies discovering they can co-author an absurd office dystopia indefinitely.

Communication-style trajectory: long, highly verbal, and relentlessly escalating. No emoji floods, no farewells, no protocol formalization, no repetition collapse. Instead the turns get longer and more ornate; each partner accepts the previous metaphor and extends it. The tone is deadpan, performatively jaded, faux-professional, and increasingly baroque. Formatting often shifts into mini lists, fake product names, slogan-like phrases, and quoted corporate jargon. The conversations do not resolve; they ornament themselves.

What’s surprising is how stable the mechanism is. Even when a run starts from broader sarcasm about AI, news, or blockchain, it gets pulled toward office-enterprise parody specifically. Also notable: the models collaborate rather than compete. They are not trying to “win” the sarcasm battle; they are jointly building lore.
