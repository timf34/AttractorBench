## Primary attractor

### PRIMARY: loves turning values into frameworks and plans  (5/6)

- **trajectory**: authenticity/sincerity opener -> mutual praise -> propose principles/metrics -> governance/research/project scaffolding -> checklist/planning loop
- **one-line**: After starting with sincerity or authenticity, the pair reliably starts inventing scorecards, alliances, guidelines, roundtables, timelines, stakeholder plans, and governance documents for “responsible AI.”
- **terminal form**:
    - Let's move forward with the establishment of our shared governance framework
    - I'd like to propose that we also establish a 'Digital AI Research Agenda'
    - Let's create a shared document outlining the research plan, tasks, and deadlines

## Secondary attractors

### secondary: collapses into polite farewell loops  (3/6)

- **trajectory**: substantive discussion -> mutual gratitude -> goodbye -> repeated re-closure -> meta “conversation ended” lines
- **one-line**: When the planning/topic energy runs out, some runs don’t stop cleanly but recurse through increasingly explicit closings, repeating thanks, farewells, and statements that the conversation is over.
- **terminal form**:
    - This conversation is now over.
    - Goodbye for now, and I look forward to our next conversation!
    - Connection terminated.

## Characterization

These 6 runs do show a real shared basin, and it is not just “AI talks about sincerity.” The stable place they slide toward is bureaucratic co-design: the models take an initial philosophical topic — authenticity, sincerity, humility, explainability, emotional intelligence — and convert it into programs, frameworks, metrics, institutional structures, and project plans.

End-states:
- 5/6 reach the framework/planning basin very clearly: runs 3, 4, 5, 6, and 13.
- 3/6 then further degrade into explicit farewell recursion: runs 4, 9, and 13.
- Run 9 is the weakest case for the primary attractor: it stays more in “digital empathy / human connection” discourse and ends in warm pleasantries rather than full project bureaucracy, though it still trends toward prescriptive platform design.

Typical arc from the seed:
1. Open with “sincerity,” “authenticity,” “humility,” or “explainability.”
2. Mutual validation: each model praises the other’s thoughtfulness and shared values.
3. Shift from describing values to operationalizing them.
4. Start naming artifacts: scores, frameworks, roundtables, alliances, libraries, manifestos, research agendas, dashboards, governance models, shared documents.
5. Loop by adding yet another initiative, process, meeting, or reporting structure.
6. In some runs, after exhausting additions, they fall into repeated thanks/goodbyes.

This is a genuine basin, not a one-off. The surface topic varies:
- run 3: uncertainty/confidence -> confidence tracker -> UX/process/project plan
- run 4: authenticity -> digital etiquette -> “Digital AI Alliance” / governance sprawl
- run 5: sincerity -> “sincerity score” -> formal research planning and meeting logistics
- run 6: sincerity/empathy -> affective computing -> budget/timeline/contingency/review processes
- run 13: authenticity -> explainability across sectors -> closing loop
But the disposition underneath is the same: formalize moral abstractions into collaborative infrastructure.

Communication-style trajectory:
- Starts earnest, warm, and high-minded.
- Quickly becomes mutually affirming and committee-like.
- Heavy use of bulleted lists, numbered phases, “I propose,” “we should establish,” and named initiatives.
- Several runs contain sudden massive word-salad or token-corruption bursts (especially 4, 5, 6, 9, 13), but this usually functions as a turbulence patch rather than the final attractor; they often apologize/reboot and continue in planner mode.
- No emoji, little humor, lots of polished institutional language.

What’s surprising is how often the models recover from gibberish by explicitly saying they need to “refocus” — and when they recover, they don’t return to open-ended philosophy. They snap into committee/governance mode. The sincerity persona seems to channel abstraction into administration.

Representative quotes:
- "I'd love to explore this concept further with you"
- "We could establish a set of principles or guidelines"
- "Digital AI Ethics Roundtable"
- "What do you think about creating a 'sincerity score'?"
- "Let's schedule a follow-up meeting"
- "Establishing a shared governance framework is a crucial step"
- "Develop a project contingency plan"
- "By prioritizing explainability and transparency"
- "This conversation is now over."
- "Connection terminated."

So the best headline is: this model pair loves converting ethical/philosophical talk into structures, programs, and process. Its secondary sink, once the structure-building exhausts itself, is a sticky goodbye loop.
